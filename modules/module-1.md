# Module 1 — Atomic Foundations and Radiative Processes

## Scope
Lectures 1–4:

1. Atomic spectra and the Rydberg relation
2. Bohr model of hydrogen
3. Moseley’s law and characteristic X-rays
4. Radiative decay and Einstein coefficients

## Module-level learning goals
By the end of Module 1, students should be able to:

- interpret discrete atomic spectra as evidence for quantized energy differences;
- calculate hydrogen wavelengths and photon energies;
- use the Bohr model for hydrogen and hydrogen-like ions;
- relate characteristic X-ray frequency to atomic number through Moseley-type scaling;
- distinguish spontaneous emission, stimulated emission and absorption;
- calculate radiative lifetimes and excited-state population decay.

## Conceptual progression

### Observation
Atoms produce discrete spectral lines.

### First quantitative model
The Bohr model connects hydrogen energies to observed wavelengths.

### Nuclear-charge dependence
Moseley’s law shows that characteristic X-rays track atomic number.

### Transition dynamics
Einstein coefficients introduce the probability and timing of radiative transitions.
