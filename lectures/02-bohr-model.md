# Lecture 2 — Bohr Model of Hydrogen

## Central question
Can a simple quantitative atomic model explain the wavelengths observed in hydrogen?

## Core relations
[
E_n=-13.6\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{eV}
]

For hydrogen:
[
r_n=a_0n^2.
]

## Worked example — (4\to2)
[
E_4=-0.85\,\mathrm{eV},qquad E_2=-3.40\,\mathrm{eV}
]
so
[
E_\gamma=2.55\,\mathrm{eV}
]
and
[
\lambda\approx\frac{1240}{2.55}\approx486\,\mathrm{nm}.
]

## Numerical practice
1. (E_3) for hydrogen: **−1.51 eV**
2. Radius for (n=3): **(4.76\times10^{-10}) m**
3. Ionization energy from (n=3): **1.51 eV**
4. Ground-state energy of He⁺: **−54.4 eV**

## Limits
The Bohr model does not adequately explain transition intensities, many-electron spectra, fine structure or the wave nature of electrons.
