# Lecture 3 — Moseley’s Law and Characteristic X-rays

## Central question
Can characteristic X-rays identify the nuclear charge of an element?

## Core idea
Moseley found approximately
[
\sqrt{\nu}\propto Z-\sigma.
]

For a simplified (K_\alpha) estimate:
[
\nu\approx\frac34Rc(Z-1)^2.
]

## Worked example — Cu (K_\alpha)
For (Z=29):
[
\nu\approx2.47\times10^{15}(28)^2
\approx1.94\times10^{18}\,\mathrm{Hz}.
]
Hence
[
\lambda\approx1.55\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}
=0.155\,\mathrm{nm},
]
and the photon energy is approximately **8.0 keV**.

## Numerical practice
1. Cu (K_\alpha) frequency: **(1.94\times10^{18}) Hz**
2. Wavelength: **0.155 nm**
3. Photon energy: **about 8.0 keV**
4. Unknown with (
u=1.50\times10^{18}\) Hz: simplified estimate **(Z\approx26)**
5. Frequency ratio for (Z=30) to (Z=20): **about 2.33**

## Significance
Moseley’s work established atomic number, not atomic mass, as the fundamental ordering variable for the periodic table.
