# Lecture 1 — Atomic Spectra and the Rydberg Relation

## Central question
Why does an excited atom emit only particular wavelengths rather than a continuous range?

## Learning outcomes
1. Distinguish continuous, emission-line and absorption-line spectra.
2. Explain why discrete spectral lines imply discrete atomic energy differences.
3. Calculate hydrogen wavelengths using the Rydberg relation.

## Core relation
[
\frac1\lambda=R_H\left(\frac1{n_f^2}-\frac1{n_i^2}\right)
]

For the Balmer series, (n_f=2).

## Worked example — Hα
For (n_i=3\to n_f=2):
[
\frac1\lambda=R_H\left(\frac14-\frac19\right)
]
so
[
\lambda\approx656\,\mathrm{nm}.
]

## Numerical practice
1. (4\to2): **486 nm**
2. (5\to2): **434 nm**
3. Balmer-series limit: **364.6 nm**
4. Photon energy at 486.1 nm: **2.55 eV**

## Misconceptions
- Photon energy equals the **difference** between two state energies.
- A spectral line is not an electron “having a colour”.
- Real sources may contain line and continuous components simultaneously.
