# Lecture 1 — Atomic Spectra and the Rydberg Relation

## Central question
Why does an excited atom emit only particular wavelengths instead of a continuous range?

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lecture, students should be able to:

1. distinguish continuous, emission-line and absorption-line spectra;
2. explain why discrete spectral lines imply discrete atomic energy differences;
3. use the Rydberg relation to calculate hydrogen wavelengths;
4. convert between wavelength, frequency and photon energy.

## Misconception checks
- The photon energy equals the **difference** between two atomic energies, not the energy of one level.
- A spectral line is not an electron “having a colour”.
- A real source can contain both line and continuous components.
- The Rydberg equation predicts wavelengths; by itself it does not explain why energy is quantized.
