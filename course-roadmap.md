# PHY368 — 32-Lecture Course Roadmap

## Block I — Atomic foundations and radiative processes
1. Atomic spectra and the Rydberg relation
2. Bohr model of hydrogen
3. Moseley’s law, atomic number and characteristic X-rays
4. Radiative decay and Einstein A and B coefficients

## Block II — Quantum hydrogen, transitions and external fields
5. Atomic units and quantum-mechanical hydrogen
6. Hydrogen eigenstates, degeneracy and radial structure
7. Dipole transitions and transition probabilities
8. Selection rules, parity, π and σ transitions
9. Orbital angular momentum and electron spin
10. Normal Zeeman effect
11. Anomalous Zeeman effect and Landé factor
12. Spin–orbit interaction
13. Hydrogen fine structure
14. Lamb shift

## Block III — Helium and many-electron atoms
15. Helium Hamiltonian and approximation models
16. Exchange symmetry and Pauli principle
17. Helium levels and spectrum
18. Shell structure and periodic table
19. Alkali atoms and quantum defects
20. Alkali fine structure and relative intensities

## Block IV — Atomic states and coupling
21. Addition of angular momenta and LS coupling
22. JJ coupling and intermediate-coupling perspective
23. Electron configurations, atomic states and term symbols
24. Fine structure and selection rules in LS coupling
25. Excited states and Rydberg atoms
26. Atomic-spectra synthesis

## Block V — Diatomic molecules and molecular spectra
27. Diatomic molecules and bonding
28. LCAO approximation and molecular orbitals
29. H₂: molecular-orbital and Heitler–London approaches
30. Electronic states and angular momentum in diatomic molecules
31. Spin multiplicity and molecular fine structure
32. Excited molecular states and spectra of diatomic molecules

## Standard lecture design
Each lecture should normally contain:
- prerequisite recall;
- one central physical question;
- derivation or quantitative model;
- one worked example;
- one active-learning task;
- misconception check;
- exit question;
- directed reading.
